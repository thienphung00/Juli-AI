# Price floor without per-SKU cost (Q1) & event uplift without a platform calendar (Q5)

**[D]** = documented in cited source · **[I]** = inferred / secondary source.

## Q1 — Discount floor when per-SKU cost is unknown

**Amazon Automate Pricing** — Six rule types (Competitive Featured Offer / Lowest Price / External Price, Based on Sales Units, two B2B). Event-driven, not scheduled. **Inputs:** a **minimum price is mandatory per rule**; max optional; explicit SKU opt-in list. **Safety:** min is a hard floor the rule may not cross; max blocks volatile upward moves. **Missing input:** the rule *cannot be activated* — Amazon makes the floor a precondition, never a fallback. **[D]** No algorithmic anti-undercut exists; race-to-the-bottom is delegated entirely to the seller-set min plus policy enforcement after the fact. https://sell.amazon.com/blog/automate-pricing-rules

**Amazon Fair Pricing / reference-price rules** — Platform-side anti-fake-discount. "Typical Price" = **90-day median price actually paid**. From 2026-04-23 a List Price must reflect real Featured-Offer sales or another retailer's price; from 2026-05-18, if >half of the 90-day history sits below the non-promotional median, Typical Price is recomputed including promo sales. **Safety:** a discount is only real against a *historically realised* price. Enforcement is account-level suspension. **[D]** https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G5TUVJKZHUVMN77V

**Repricer.com (representative 3P)** — Min/max set manually, bulk, CSV, or auto-calculated via Buy Box Threshold, **Net Margin** (needs cost), or **Item Cost/MAP** (cost or RRP ± fixed/%). **Safety:** layered floors — the tool's min, plus the seller's Seller-Central min/max as a **final safety net the software will not overwrite** (Amazon deactivates the listing if price escapes that band); a "Profit Margin Guard" master-overrides all competitive rules; "Sleep Mode" pauses repricing daily; cooling periods between adjustments prevent penny wars. **Missing input:** no-min SKUs sit *outside* automation (bulk tools target "products that haven't yet had a Min/Max assigned"); where cost is absent the fallback floors are non-cost — % off RRP, MAP, or Buy-Box threshold. **[D/I]** https://support.repricer.com/minimum-and-maximum-prices · https://www.repricer.com/blog/setting-min-max-prices-repricing/

**Prisync** — Five competitor-relative positions (Cheapest→Most Expensive), IF-THIS-THEN-THAT rules resolved by hierarchy (product/brand beats category). **Safety:** "Once you define your product costs, you can set a minimum price **or profit margin**." Floor is **optional and cost-gated**. **Missing input:** no cost ⇒ no margin floor at all; rule runs purely competitor-relative. This permissive design is exactly the failure Juli's shop cap prevents. **[D/I]** https://prisync.com/features/repricing-dynamic-pricing-rules/

**Shopee Seller Centre** — Discount Promotion tool: **max 90-day duration**, 1,000 products/promotion, per-buyer purchase limit at product (not variation) level. **A too-high discount triggers an automatic warning before commit** ("to ensure the discount amount was not input wrongly") — a typo guard, not a margin floor. While a promotion is active the base price is **locked**: external syncs cannot change it, the seller must adjust discount depth instead. Separately, misleading/inflated original prices are a prohibited-listing offence. **Missing input:** no cost input exists; Shopee never attempts a margin floor. **[D/I]** https://seller.shopee.com.my/edu/article/3709 · https://shopee-channel.helpscoutdocs.com/article/93-syncing-limitations-when-shopee-product-is-under-promotion

**TikTok Shop Price Diagnosis / Smart Pricing Suite** — Seller Center → Products → Price Diagnosis splits SKUs into Uncompetitive/Competitive vs "similar products on the platform", recommends a price; seller applies Product Discount (fixed price, SKU level) or Seller Voucher (SPU level), both defaulting to **30-day validity**. Pricing Suite adds a simulator computing true stacked checkout price across seller discount + platform coupon + campaign. **Safety: none on the downside — no minimum price, no max-discount %, no cooldown, no change-frequency limit are documented.** Only upside is stated (traffic, orders, subsidy voucher eligibility), and the FAQ is explicit: **"There is no penalty if you don't change prices according to recommendations."** **Missing input:** the recommendation is a black box over platform comparables and never sees seller cost — it can recommend below-cost. **[D]** https://seller-my.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=7563289899878146&default_language=en

**Lazada** — No retrievable seller-facing documentation of a Suggested-price / Price-Compare floor or cap. **Unverified — do not cite as precedent. [I]**

**Floor definitions in use:** cost+margin (needs cost: Repricer Net Margin, Prisync) · MAP/RRP ± % (no cost) · % off list (no cost) · competitor-relative only (Prisync default, TikTok) · rolling median of realised price (Amazon Typical Price, as reference) · absolute seller-set min (Amazon, **mandatory**). Recurring guardrails: per-SKU opt-in; a floor that supersedes every other rule; a second floor at the platform layer the tool can't overwrite; daily pause windows and cooldowns; a warning interstitial on abnormal depth; duration caps (Shopee 90d, TikTok 30d).

### What Juli should borrow
1. **Make the shop-level cap mandatory before any discount card can be approved** — Amazon's no-min-no-automation beats a silent default. Agent proposes, seller confirms once; that confirmation *is* the floor.
2. **Two independent floors, take the tighter:** shop max-discount % off reference, and an absolute VND floor per SKU when supplied. Compute reference as the **rolling 90-day median realised unit price from the shop's own orders**, not list price — anti-inflated-strike-through, needs no cost.
3. **Master-override semantics:** the floor supersedes every proposal path; clamp *and* say so on the card, never surface a sub-floor number.
4. **Cooldown + duration cap:** one price change per SKU per N days; refuse a second write in the window rather than queueing — fits single-write approval exactly.
5. **Deep-discount interstitial** at ~half the cap — Shopee's typo guard, cheap, catches unit/zero errors.
6. **With cost unknown, do not synthesise a margin floor.** Every tool lacking cost falls back to a non-cost floor. Label the card honestly: "floor = your shop cap, not a margin floor."

### Contradicting evidence
- **A single shop-level % is materially weaker than per-SKU minimums.** Amazon and Repricer operate at SKU granularity precisely because a blanket % lets thin-margin SKUs go underwater while over-constraining fat ones. The per-SKU override is not polish — it is what makes the shop cap safe; the agent should actively nudge for it on high-velocity, low-price SKUs. **[I]**
- **% off *list price* is the wrong base.** If list is inflated or the SKU is perpetually discounted, "max 20% off" authorises a price below anything realised. Amazon rewrote its entire reference-price rule in 2026 over this. Use the realised-price median. **[D]**
- **The agent-proposed default cap is the riskiest element.** No platform proposes a floor on the seller's behalf; every one requires the seller to state it, or has no floor. Defensible only if conservative, visibly labelled a guess, and affirmatively confirmed — never silently accepted. **[D→I]**

## Q5 — Event uplift for replenishment without a platform calendar

**TikTok Shop inventory management** — Surfaces total/available/locked stock, sales forecast, suggested restock, days of supply, alerts. Restock is an explicit deterministic formula: **`(sales_forecast_volume × time_interval) − current_available_stock`**, intervals **7/14/21/30/45/60 days**. Days of supply = days current stock covers at current velocity. Alerts per SKU or bulk on qty or days-of-supply, firing **once daily** max. **Safety:** determinism — the seller can reproduce it by hand. **Degrades:** thin history propagates linearly; nothing documented for new SKUs; **no campaign/event input exists at all** — 9.9/11.11 uplift is unmodelled. **[D]** https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/managing-inventory-orders-and-promotions-in-tiktok-shop-seller-center/

**Amazon Restock Inventory / FBA capacity** — Per-ASIN recommended quantity + ship date from sales history, **seller-entered lead time**, and proprietary demand signals; refreshed daily. **Safety:** guidance is a **days-of-supply band**, not a point forecast — ~30–60 days target; **>90 days trips excess-inventory flags and aged-inventory surcharges**, an explicit *upper* guard most seller tools omit. **Events:** no per-event multiplier is exposed to sellers; Amazon handles events at the **capacity layer** — restock limits sized to ~5 months of sales including Prime Day (cut from 6), ASIN-level limits introduced before Prime Day 2025, capacity tied to IPI/velocity, hard shipment cut-offs weeks ahead. **Degrades:** acknowledged to be good on stable products and to **miss trend inflection points** — precisely the event case. **[D/I]** https://www.sellerlabs.com/blog/amazon-restock-limits-2026-strategy/ · https://www.ecomengine.com/blog/fba-capacity-limits

**Shopee** — No retrievable seller-facing Stock Forecast tool tying inventory suggestions to 9.9/11.11; the campaign surface is registration + discount mechanics, not forecasting. **Unverified. [I]**

**Inventory Planner** — An **Edit Forecast** screen overrides forecast units, revenue, or applies a **percentage adjustment** for a period — a manual multiplier on a statistical baseline, layered over category (Open-to-Buy) overrides. **Safety:** the override is explicit, period-bounded, and visible *as* an override; the baseline survives underneath. **Degrades:** without an override, pure baseline — an unflagged event silently under-forecasts. **[D]** https://help.inventory-planner.com/en/articles/1117129-edit-forecast

**RELEX (reference implementation)** — ML over **promotional master data**: promotion type (e.g. multi-buy), price change/discount depth, advertising strategy, display changes, combined with baseline seasonality and weekday patterns; learns factor **interactions**, and models **cannibalisation and halo** (promoting A suppresses substitute B) alongside the promoted item's lift. **Inputs:** a promotion calendar with per-promotion attributes, sales history, inventory/DC capacity. **Safety:** exception-based review — planners see only what the system **flags as exceptional, e.g. novel promotion types**. **Degrades:** no-history promotions are explicitly handed to humans; no post-event self-calibration is described. **[D]** https://www.relexsolutions.com/resources/promotion-forecasting-and-replenishment/

**Demand-planning literature** — Standard decomposition `promo demand = baseline × lift`, baseline being the counterfactual. Practice: isolate promo demand first and forecast uplift **separately** — never blend promo periods into baseline history, or the baseline inflates and the next non-promo forecast over-orders. Lift is estimated from comparable past promotions matched on **discount depth, timing, category, visibility**. **Documented failure mode: static uplift factors are called unreliable** — "assuming a 15% discount always generates a 25% increase" fails because the effect varies by depth, timing, category and advertising. **Post-event calibration:** compare actual to forecast and **update the lift library**; `Post-Promotion Factor = Actual Post-Promo Sales / Baseline Expected Sales` also captures the post-event dip from pull-forward. **Safety stock:** promo periods carry higher forecast variance, so the buffer must widen for the event window and revert after. **[D/I]** https://www.algo.com/blog/why-demand-forecasting-misses-promotion-effects/ · https://www.planster.io/blog/forecast-adjustment-promotions · https://www.tredence.com/blog/decoding-the-metrics-a-deep-dive-into-calculating-promotion-effectiveness

### What Juli should borrow
1. **Keep TikTok's formula as the spine; make the event a multiplier on the forecast term only:** `restock = (baseline_daily × uplift × horizon + event_buffer) − available`. Hand-reproducible, and degrades to TikTok's own documented formula at `uplift = 1.0`.
2. **Exclude prior event windows from the baseline** — the most-cited error in the literature. If 11.11 sits in the trailing-30-day window the baseline is already inflated and multiplying double-counts. Mask historical spike windows before fitting.
3. **Deterministic uplift hierarchy, stated on the card:** this SKU's prior-event multiplier → same-category multiplier from this shop → conservative category default → 1.0. Show which tier fired and its n.
4. **Add an upper guard**, not just a lower one — Amazon's >90-days-of-supply excess flag is the guardrail seller tools skip. Cap proposed restock at max days-of-supply; over-ordering into a weak event is the expensive failure.
5. **Widen the buffer for the event window only**, then revert; do not raise standing safety stock.
6. **Post-event calibration:** write back `actual / (baseline × horizon)` as the SKU's realised multiplier so the next event uses tier 1. Each approved card becomes training data with no ML. Capture the **post-event dip** too, so the next replenishment doesn't over-order into the trough.
7. **Exception-based review:** flag only weak-evidence uplifts (category default, or n=1), rather than asking the seller to confirm every number.

### Contradicting evidence
- **"Prior-event multiplier else category default" is exactly the static-factor pattern the literature calls unreliable.** Soft contradiction: static factors fail because they ignore **discount depth**. Condition the uplift on this card's depth, or restrict it to comparable-depth priors — a prior 11.11 at 50% off will badly over-forecast a 15%-off event. **[D]**
- **One prior event is a sample of one.** Report n on the card; prefer the category default at n=1 with extreme deviation.
- **Cannibalisation is unmodelled.** RELEX treats the promoted SKU's lift and its substitutes' decline as one problem. Promoting one SKU while replenishing its substitute at baseline over-stocks the substitute. Likely out of v1 scope — state it as a known limitation, not an oversight.
- **No platform precedent for agent-proposed event dates.** TikTok and Amazon expose no event input; Inventory Planner and RELEX take the calendar as seller-supplied master data. Spike-detected dates are genuinely novel, so the card needs the strongest evidence display (which past spikes, what dates, what magnitude) — there is no upstream source of truth to check against. **[D→I]**
- **Baseline and promotional demand must be separate in the data model**, not just in the calculation. If Juli stores only a blended forecast, the calibration loop above is not implementable later without a migration.
