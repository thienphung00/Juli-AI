# ADR-087: Subject-scoped Action Cards, card revisions, and a per-visitor demo tenant

**Status:** Proposed
**Date:** 2026-08-26
**Deciders:** grill-with-docs (Architect) with owner
**Amends:** ADR-082 decision 1, ADR-084 decision 1. Schedules ADR-083's deferred subject-scoping T-slice.

> **Numbering:** assumes [ADR-086](086-runtime-database-role-and-tenant-isolation.md) lands first.
> If it does not, renumber — ADR-083's own header records a prior collision of exactly this kind.

## Context

The functional requirement: **a fully executed Action Card must not be regenerated by
Manual Refresh.** If the signals later justify a *better* pass at the same subject, the
next card should be a successor that carries the previous one forward — "Optimize Product
for product 1001, second pass, referencing the first".

That sentence is not expressible in the current schema, and verifying why turned up four
coupled decisions.

**Today's identity is `(shop, workflow_key)`.** `uq_action_cards_shop_workflow` is a plain
UNIQUE on `(shop_id, workflow_key)`. The card carries no product and no revision. Per
ADR-082 decision 1 the product is derived server-side *at approval time* from the highest
`products.revenue`, so a card is a shop-level offer whose subject is unknown until someone
approves it. With that identity, "do not regenerate an executed card" degenerates into
**"never offer Optimize Product to this shop again"**, because there is only ever one.

**One shared demo tenant burns its cards.** `approval.py:214` requires `status == "active"`
and `:223` sets `"approved"`, with no path back. Combined with the unique constraint, the
first visitor to approve consumes that card for every visitor after them. This is observable
on the deployed database today: of the sandbox shop's three cards,
`create_hero_product_1` and `process_order_5` are already `approved` from the #1226 gate
walks, leaving one. `uq_workflow_runs_active_shop_product` independently rejects a second
concurrent visitor on the same `(shop, product)` with `concurrency_conflict` — correct for
one seller protecting themselves, wrong for two strangers.

**Only one workflow has a resolvable subject.** Across every shop on the deployed database,
including the production merchant with 116 products, `inventory_items`, `orders`,
`campaigns`, `returns` and `livestreams` are all **empty**. Of the eleven surfaced workflow
keys (`MID_LARGE_WORKFLOW_KEYS`), only `optimize_product_2` can bind a subject that exists.

## Decisions

1. **Action Cards become subject-scoped.** Identity widens from `(shop, workflow_key)` to
   `(shop_id, workflow_key, subject_type, subject_id)`, the shape ADR-083 and `CONTEXT.md`
   already name — *"a card is `(shop, category, subject)`"*. The subject is polymorphic
   because Optimize Product's subject is a product and Process Order's is an order.

   This **amends ADR-082 decision 1**: product binding moves from approval-time revenue
   derivation to card-generation time. The revenue heuristic existed because the card had no
   subject; once it has one, deriving a different product at approval would contradict the
   card the seller was shown.

2. **Two constraints, not one.** A full unique on
   `(shop_id, workflow_key, subject_type, subject_id, revision)` for the chain, plus a
   **partial** unique on the same tuple minus `revision` `WHERE status` is active — at most
   one live card per subject per workflow. `workflow_key` stays in both keys deliberately:
   ADR-083 permits two agents on the same product under *different* categories, and a key
   on subject alone would forbid the case that ADR is explicit about allowing.

   `uq_workflow_runs_active_shop_product` is re-keyed in the same slice. ADR-083 already
   calls it *"too coarse — it blocks the allowed cross-category case."*

3. **Revisions are chained rows, not a counter.** A successor is a new row with
   `supersedes_card_id` pointing at its predecessor. The previous revision's information is
   reached by **reference**, never copied forward: a duplicated fact that can drift is the
   failure ADR-085 rejected on the same grounds.

   The column must not be named in a way that collides with `workflow_key`'s trailing
   identifiers — `optimize_product_2`, `process_order_5b` and `replenish_inventory_3b` carry
   catalog suffixes, not versions, and a bare `version` column guarantees future readers
   conflate the two.

4. **`create_*` workflows are out of scope.** `create_hero_product_1` and
   `create_activity_7a` act on something that does not exist yet, so they have no subject to
   scope to. They are excluded rather than special-cased: the other nine workflows act on
   entities that already exist in the merchant's shop before they ever connect to Juli, so
   every `subject_id` is a real foreign key with no nullable branch. A nullable subject
   would put the uniqueness this ADR depends on beyond what a constraint can enforce.

5. **The subject taxonomy is Product (including inventory), Campaign, Order, and
   After-sales.** Verified against the catalog rather than assumed:
   Product covers `optimize_product_2`, `replenish_inventory_3`, `clear_excess_4`;
   Campaign covers `update_activity_7c`, `delete_activity_7b`;
   Order covers `process_order_5`;
   After-sales covers `prevent_cancellation_8a`, `prevent_return_8b`, `prevent_refund_8c`,
   which share one subject and are correctly combined.

   Inventory hangs off Product rather than `inventory_items`, which holds zero rows
   everywhere. **Livestream and Customer Service are reserved, not modelled** — they have
   tables and a `csat` KPI but map to **no** workflow keys (`KPI_WORKFLOW_KEYS["csat"] = ()`).

6. **A revision is legitimate when the subject's basis has changed since the last executed
   revision's snapshot; otherwise the card is suppressed with a named reason.** The
   machinery exists: ADR-083's Basis snapshot guard, `workflow_runs.state.basis_snapshots`,
   and `action_card_approvals.card_snapshot`. What counts as *material* change is defined
   per workflow key in the catalog, not globally — a 2% price move and a stock level
   crossing zero are not the same event. `suppressed_reason` already exists for the negative
   case.

   **Outcome-based revision is rejected for now**, not on principle: `impact_readings` holds
   **0** rows and ADR-077's gate stays open until a real one exists, so gating revisions on
   measured impact would emit no revision, ever. Revisit when readings exist. A time-based
   rule is admissible only as a secondary cap on churn, never as the primary trigger — it
   manufactures the appearance of an improvement on a schedule.

7. **A replayed demo run applies its captured outcome to its own tenant's rows.** Per
   ADR-084 decision 2 a demo run seeds captured events as real `workflow_run_events`; nothing
   reaches TikTok, so the subject's basis would otherwise be identical before and after, and
   decision 6 would suppress every successor — the demo could never show the second pass it
   exists to demonstrate. Applying the recorded, masked outcome locally makes `executed_at`
   true rather than cosmetic and lets **one rule serve both the demo and live paths**, with
   no demo-only branch in the emission path to drift out of step.

   The rejected alternative is a scenario set that encodes the progression by hand, which
   edges into what ADR-084 decision 2 prohibits by name: *"Hand-authored event JSON is not an
   acceptable input anywhere in this pipeline."* It remains the quarantined fallback for any
   workflow whose outcome is not representable as a row change on our side.

8. **The demo tenant becomes per-visitor.** Each anonymous session is seeded its own shop,
   not merely its own `users` row. This **amends ADR-084 decision 1**, which says "its own
   seeded tenant" in the singular.

   **Per-visitor is not merely preferable — it is the only option that does not require
   inventing shop membership.** `get_active_shop` (`api/dependencies.py:28`) resolves
   `X-Shop-Id` against `ShopsRepo.list(user.id)` — shops *owned by* the authenticated user —
   and raises `403 "Shop not accessible"` otherwise. An anonymous visitor owns zero shops, so
   a single `DEMO_SHOP_ID` yields 403 on every authenticated demo route no matter what is
   seeded. #1313's branch does not close this: its diff contains no shop logic. The only
   alternative is a `shop_members` table plus a rewrite of the ownership model, which
   re-creates the many-users-one-shop arrangement decision 8 exists to avoid.

   **Consequence for #1313:** per-visitor provisioning is a *prerequisite* of that issue, not
   a follow-on. Seeding the template tenant alone unblocks nothing.

   Isolation becomes structural rather than guarded: no shared card to burn, no shared
   product row for decision 7 to collide on, and no shared active-run key — so the
   concurrency guard still protects one visitor from double-approving while never firing
   between strangers. It also removes the need for a named policy exception admitting
   `DEMO_SHOP_ID`, recorded in [ADR-086](086-runtime-database-role-and-tenant-isolation.md):
   the anonymous user **owns** their own shop, so
   `shops.user_id = current_setting('app.current_user_id')` matches and RLS admits them with
   no special case.

9. **The emission budget is left keyed as it is; `max_active` is the constraint that moves.**
   Verified in `services/action_cards/emission_budget.py` rather than assumed:

   - **Cooldown (gate 1, hard) needs no change.** `_in_cooldown` reads *the card's own*
     terminal timestamps via `_terminal_marker(card)`. It is per-card, not a lookup across
     cards by `workflow_key`. Under subject-scoping, per-card becomes per-subject for free —
     and it doubles as the secondary time-based floor decision 6 wants, so a basis change
     inside 7 days still waits.
   - **The novelty ledger stays keyed `(shop_id, week_start, workflow_key)`.** The weekly cap
     is *soft* — a churn target that only ranks candidates and "by itself never removes a
     candidate from the surfaced set". Adding the subject to its key would make every product
     count as separately novel, which drains the quota's meaning: it exists to say "this
     *kind* of Decision was new to the seller this week", and that is workflow-level by
     nature.
   - **`max_active` (default 5) is the real ceiling, and subject-scoping is what strains it.**
     It is the only hard supply limit. A shop with 116 products can produce 116 Optimize
     candidates where it produced one, and five surface. Nothing breaks — but *which* five is
     now a ranking problem the current `ORDER BY priority, workflow_key` was never designed
     to solve, because it has no way to prefer one product over another within a key.
     Subject-level ranking is in scope for the slice that lands decision 1; raising
     `max_active` is not the fix, since the cap protects seller attention.

## Consequences

- **Subject resolution fails closed.** A signal that cannot resolve its subject suppresses
  the card with a named reason rather than emitting a dangling one. `signals.py:271` already
  models this posture, returning `_unavailable_kpi(..., reason="Chưa đồng bộ tồn kho")`
  rather than inventing a number.
- **Only `optimize_product_2` is provable today.** Order, Campaign and After-sales cards
  cannot bind a subject until ingest populates those tables. The design is correct for all
  nine; the acceptance evidence can only cover one until then, and slices should say so
  rather than assert coverage they cannot demonstrate.
- **Per-visitor tenancy needs a reaper**, and its predicate must be one that cannot match a
  real shop. Row growth is a shop, its products and its cards per session.
- **`services/seeds/demo_tenant.py` becomes template-based.** It currently derives one
  deterministic shop id and one user id from `DEMO_SHOP_ID`; per-visitor seeding is a real
  change to that module, not a configuration flip.
- **Prerequisite: the demo tenant does not exist on the deployed host.** `DEMO_SHOP_ID` is
  unset and no "Demo Shop" row exists, so #1312's seed has never run there. Nothing in this
  ADR is verifiable in the demo until it does.
- Migration touches a table with live rows and changes a unique constraint, so the existing
  7 cards need a recorded backfill: their subject is unknown, which is the point.

## Options considered

| Alternative | Why rejected |
| --- | --- |
| Keep `(shop, workflow_key)`, put the subject in `metadata_json` | The uniqueness the requirement depends on would live in a text blob no constraint can enforce; "one live card per subject" becomes application discipline. The same present-looking, non-functional shape as the dead RLS policies in ADR-086 |
| A `version` counter incremented on one row | The predecessor's information is destroyed by the update, which is the half of the requirement that says the successor must carry it |
| Copy the previous revision's payload into the successor | A duplicated fact that drifts; rejected on the same grounds as ADR-085's denormalised tenancy column |
| Scope `create_*` to the product category being filled | `products.category` is empty on every deployed row, so the subject would be null in practice — a constraint unenforceable on real data |
| Gate revisions on measured impact | `impact_readings` is empty and ADR-077's gate is open; no revision would ever be emitted |
| Shared demo tenant with a scheduled re-seed | Cheaper, but two visitors inside one window still collide — shipping a known race |
| Shared demo tenant with non-mutating replay | Avoids the third contention point but leaves the first two: the first visitor still burns the cards, and the demo stays single-use across all visitors |
